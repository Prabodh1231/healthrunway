HealthRunway

Medical-shock early warning for households, with a generic-medicine savings finder
DSH Hacks V2 · AI x Healthcare
Web app · Next.js · Claude

The problem

In India, most hospital and medicine bills are paid from the family's own pocket. A single admission can wipe out savings and push a household into debt. The WHO calls health spending above 10% of household income catastrophic (SDG 3.8.2). Families rarely see it coming, and many keep paying 5–10× more than they need to for the long-term medicines they take every day.

What we built

1. Two-minute risk check. Covers family members, ages, long-term conditions, income, savings, insurance and regular health costs. Data stays in the browser: no accounts, no server copy.
2. Early-warning report. The health runway is the month the family's expected health spending crosses the WHO 10% line. A hospital shock test splits a major bill into what insurance pays, what savings cover and the unpaid gap. A 0–100 risk score shows what drives it.
3. What-if and next steps. Toggle "switch to generics", "₹5 lakh cover" or "save 10% of income" and watch the score update live. Next steps link to real schemes: PM-JAY, Ayushman Vay Vandana (all 70+) and Jan Aushadhi Kendras.
4. Medicine savings finder. Photograph a prescription or search a brand. Each medicine is matched to the Jan Aushadhi generic with the same salts and strengths. Savings feed back into the risk score.

How AI is used

  • Prescription reading: Claude (vision + structured output) reads printed or handwritten prescriptions, including Indian shorthand (1-0-1, BD, HS), into medicine, strength and doses per day.
  • Plain-language plan in English or Hindi, written only from computed figures and never giving medical advice.
  • Not AI: prices, matching and risk scores are deterministic and traceable to sources. Both AI features have offline fallbacks.

Data and clinical safety

  • 96 Jan Aushadhi chronic-care generics (official MRP list) matched to ~22,600 branded listings. Salt names are normalised and price is compared per tablet.
  • Regular vs extended-release differences are flagged. Thyroxine and anti-epileptics carry a "switch only with your doctor" warning. Combination medicines prompt a strip check.
  • Every model assumption is listed in the app's How it works page.

Impact and next steps

HealthRunway turns a national statistic, catastrophic health spending, into a personal early warning a family can act on today, starting with the cheapest lever: the medicines they already buy every month. Next steps: calibrate hospitalisation rates with NSS age tables and PM-JAY claims, extend to the full 2,400-product Jan Aushadhi list, add a Kendra locator, track real monthly spending, and support more Indian languages.
